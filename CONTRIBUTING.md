# Contributing

ChezCat welcomes ordinary bug reports, compatibility observations and feature suggestions. Submit ordinary issues through the official repository. Use the private security-reporting route for sensitive findings.

## Feedback versus material for inclusion

Non-code suggestions and original explanatory text submitted as feedback may be used as described in CSAPUL Section 8.1. An idea does not give its author an ownership interest in the project, and the project need not implement or pay for it.

Code patches, substantial executable tests, documentation and artwork proposed for inclusion require the [separate Contributor Copyright Assignment and Patent Agreement](licensing/CONTRIBUTOR_AGREEMENT.md). A minimal reproducer attached to a bug report may be evaluated as evidence, but incorporating its protected code into the product or test suite requires the applicable inclusion rights; calling it “feedback” is not a shortcut.

## Public versus private submissions

Public issues and code pull requests are publicly visible and subject to GitHub's applicable content, forking and inbound-license terms. Rejection does not recall copies or undo platform permissions already granted. Source-only contribution forks are permitted under CSAPUL 8.2A for proposing and evaluating a Submission. They may remain as historical source records after acceptance, rejection or withdrawal, but not as independently distributed products. Use an unofficial-status notice, identify the maintainer and summarize changes in the fork README. This is not permission for off-platform public mirrors, release binaries, package publication or hosted services. Separately permitted private clones, builds and tests remain allowed. Public submissions are not confidential and are not ownership transfers. Do not submit confidential material publicly. Arrange a private route before sending it, and use the security route for sensitive vulnerability details.

GitHub's Terms of Service, effective April 27, 2026, address provider and affiliate content permissions in Section D.4 and AI-feature data use/opt-out in Section J.3. The J.3 opt-out does not withdraw the separate D.4–D.8 grants or apply generally to public-repository content. Review the current applicable terms at https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-of-service before posting and ensure You have authority for those platform uses. This warning is not an additional general AI-training grant by CSAPUL. Private material requires separate review of the actual account agreement, feature settings and authorized evaluation systems.

## Review and acceptance process

1. Identify the proposed change and its authorship, rights holder, immutable revision or patch digest, and any existing material it includes.

2. Obtain and retain the contributor's explicit evaluation assent under C2 before copying, executing or modifying substantive material beyond independently applicable platform permissions. The submission form or an authenticated written acknowledgement must identify the exact C2 terms/version or digest, immutable submission revision/digest, assenting rights holder or authorized representative, and date. Opening a pull request is not itself recorded C2 assent. Record it before the maintainer or review/CI systems exercise that evaluation permission; do not infer it from running a workflow. Limited bug-reproducer evaluation has the separate permission in CSAPUL 8.1. Public viewing and inbound platform licensing do not replace signed assignment.

3. Review engineering quality and provenance. The maintainer may reject, request changes or accept only a stated portion.

4. Complete Schedule A for agreement version 1.1 and obtain signatures from every necessary rights holder or authorized representative and the maintainer's countersigned acceptance. For electronic signing, preserve explicit electronic-transaction consent, the exact text and schedule hashes, identity/authority evidence, date and audit trail, and make the executed copy available to both parties. An authenticated typed signature can be used only through that documented process; merely typing a handle into a PR is not it. No paid signing provider is required by this policy. Do not merge substantive code into the maintained product or tests before execution and acceptance.

5. Preserve the signed record privately. Record the public credit name/handle and accepted contribution description in CONTRIBUTORS.md and preserve accurate Git authorship.

6. Changes to the accepted scope require an updated record; the agreement is not an unlimited grab of future work.

## Evaluation acknowledgement — unexecuted template

The following is an acknowledgement template, not an acknowledgement by any actual contributor:

> I have reviewed and agree to the evaluation-only permission in Section C2 of the ChezCat Contributor Copyright Assignment and Patent Agreement, agreement version/text digest [identify exact text], for Submission [immutable revision or patch digest]. I am the identified rights holder or am authorized to grant that permission. I understand that a public submission is public and subject to applicable platform terms. This evaluation acknowledgement does not transfer copyright or accept my work into the project. Copyright assignment, the license-back and the contribution patent grants require the separate executed agreement and countersigned acceptance of Schedule A.

Complete the identifiers before presenting the acknowledgement. Retain the actual response, sender/authority evidence and date; do not store unnecessary personal details in public. A checked box is evidence only to the extent the surrounding process establishes legally sufficient assent and authority. A new material revision must be covered by an appropriate updated record.

The accepted original copyright transfers to Adam Maddox under the signed agreement. Contributors receive the narrow license-back for their own separable work only; it does not grant existing ChezCat code. Contributor patent permission is a separate grant. Contributors keep appropriate historical credit but receive no veto, royalty entitlement, promise of permanent inclusion or automatic official-branch status.

The maintainer must document relevant human authorship, employment/third-party rights and any tool-assisted material before representing that ownership has transferred. A blanket assertion that the project owns every generated line is not sufficient.

For a public fork, applicable GitHub rights remain in effect. Forking is neither an assignment to the maintainer nor an unrestricted license to commercialize the browser. Do not upload credentials, sensitive browsing captures, personal data or material whose owner has not authorized submission.

## Intake controls and privacy

External substantive contribution execution remains gated on recorded C2 assent
and an approved exact immutable submission revision. Routine trusted-push CI is
separate. Do not run untrusted contributor code with secrets, release permissions
or on a maintainer's personal runner. Require approval for external execution,
including returning contributors; a changed revision is a new execution decision.
Do not mistake a green run for permission or a signature.

Until the signing and private-record workflow is operationally verified, accept
ordinary reports and limited report reproducers only; do not invite substantive
contributions for automatic evaluation or maintained integration. Owner-authored
work and independent development are not blocked by that intake restriction.
The permission for a compliant source contribution fork remains governed by the
license, not by whether the maintainer is currently accepting contributions.

Executed documents, identity/authority evidence and private contact details stay
private. Public records contain chosen credit and the accepted work's scope, not
signatures or private addresses. See [contributor privacy](docs/CONTRIBUTOR_PRIVACY.md).
