# Contributor privacy and legal records

Licensing and record questions: legal@bitmadd.com. This notice describes the
project's contribution-record process; it does not replace GitHub's own terms or
privacy notice. Do not send passwords, identity-document scans, home addresses or
unnecessary personal information with a report or pull request.

For a substantive contribution, the maintainer needs the rights holder's identity
and signing capacity, a private notice contact, necessary authority/provenance
evidence, exact submission and agreement identities, evaluation assent, signatures
and an execution audit trail. Collect only what is reasonably needed to establish
those rights and administer the agreement. Do not collect an identification number
or unrelated personal records as a routine condition of contributing.

Keep executed records and contact details in access-controlled private storage
with protected backups. Access is limited to the maintainer and specifically
authorized people or service providers with a necessary role, subject to applicable
confidentiality and law. No new paid signature provider or unrestricted external
AI review is implied. Give both signing parties access to the executed record.

Retain the minimum core evidence of assignments, license-backs, patent grants and
material notices while those rights are relied on and for documented legal-claim
or preservation needs. Review necessity at least annually. Delete or anonymize
unnecessary ancillary identity material and raw correspondence once its purpose
ends, subject to a documented preservation hold. Rejected-submission material is
not retained as a product contribution; only reasonably necessary private review,
security and rights records may be retained. This is a necessity-based policy,
not a claim that a statute requires a fixed universal retention period.

Public credit uses the agreed name or handle and work description. Do not publish
signatures, private notice addresses, authority documents or audit-trail exports.
A public submission remains subject to public platform visibility and previously
granted rights; the project cannot promise deletion from all forks, caches or
third-party copies. Request a credit correction or discuss private-record access
through the designated contact; identity and legal-retention constraints apply.

Ordinary browser-license acceptance is a different process. Its official tools
keep a minimal receipt locally; they do not require an account, send the receipt
to the maintainer, or register users on a licensing server.
