Use “ChezCat” as the product attribution. Internal/private Modified Builds may retain the project name and official logo within CSAPUL Section 5, with an “Unofficial modified build” disclosure, maintainer identity and change information. Monetized demonstrations may disclose that status in an appropriate caption, notice or spoken statement rather than every frame. Audience questions do not alone convert a presenter-operated explanation into a hosted service. Audience remote control, browser-job APIs or equivalent service access are different.

This is no claim of trademark registration and no independent distribution permission. Public officially branded branches need exact written approval under Section 7.1, with quality-control, review, security, duration and suspension terms. Keep authentic historical endorsement records. Do not invent an approval or remove credit to imply ownership of the base project.

## Reasonable standards for permitted Modified Builds

A build using the name or project logo must clearly say “Unofficial modified
build,” identify its maintainer and provide a concise description of material
changes. It must not claim unsupported compatibility, an unperformed security
review, current official endorsement or ownership of the base project. Preserve
copyright and third-party notices. Disclose material weakening of security or
privacy defaults rather than presenting a modified build as the official one.
Do not use branding to conceal malicious behavior or mislead about origin,
maintenance, supported versions or security.

The licensor may give a written notice under LICENSE section 5 requiring a
specific noncompliant or misleading Modified Build to stop using the name/logo.
Retain accurate historical attribution and replace misleading branding with a
neutral identifier; loss of branding permission alone does not revoke otherwise
valid software grants. These standards create no general distribution permission
and do not suppress independently lawful criticism, review or descriptive reference.
